# Why Sift

Sift isn't for sale. This page just says what it is for and what it does that the usual apps don't.

## A notes app you're allowed to be messy in

Most of us already have a notes app full of half-thoughts: a phone number with no name, "ring about the boiler", a shopping list from March, an idea at 2 a.m. Notes apps treat every one of those as a document you're meant to keep tidy. So they pile up, you stop trusting the pile, and you start a new one somewhere else.

Sift's **Brain Dump** starts from the other end. It expects you to empty your head quickly and sort it out later, or never:

- **Writing comes first.** Open the app and you're on Brain Dump with the cursor in a New note. On a phone the note fills the screen, with nothing moving behind it, until you press Done.
- **A brain dump can be a passing thing, and it's still kept for good.** When a line becomes a task, a contact or a plan for the day, the note isn't emptied or thrown away. The words stay where you wrote them and turn into a link to the task or contact they became. That task or contact links back to the note. You never have to decide whether it's safe to delete the note.
- **Nothing is lost by tidying up.** Every change can be undone, in any order. Archived things stay searchable, and the Bin keeps deleted things for 30 days.
- **The mess sorts itself a little.** A phone number typed into any note becomes a contact you can call from, with a record of when you looked it up and when you rang. Long first lines become short titles on their own. The most recently edited notes rise to the top.

## Compared with Apple Notes and Google Keep

Apple Notes and Google Keep are good at keeping notes. Sift is for what happens to a note after you've written it.

| | Apple Notes | Google Keep | Sift |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quick capture, checklists, photos | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Turn a line into a task or a contact, and keep the line | No (copy it by hand) | No (a reminder at most) | Yes: ↗ Make, and the line becomes a link |
| A phone number in a note is a real contact, with a call log | Tap to call only | Tap to call only | Yes |
| Your day on paper: tasks given times and lengths, unfinished things carried over | No | No | Day Planner |
| Energy levels: fit today's tasks to how you feel | No | No | Yes |
| Where things are kept (boxes, shelves, search inside a box) | No | No | Find Things |
| Ongoing sagas: every call, letter and task about one complaint or claim in one timeline | No | No | Cases |
| Undo any change, in any order | Recent edits only | Recent edits only | Full history |
| Where your data lives | Apple's iCloud (end-to-end encryption if you turn it on) | Google's servers | Your devices, and optionally your own server, always end-to-end encrypted |
| Your own words for the app's names and headings | No | No | Settings → Your words |

## The unusual bits

- **One place for a life's loose ends.** Brain Dump, Tasks, the Day Planner, Lists, Find Things, Contacts and Cases share one notes editor and link to each other. A 📞 in any note links a contact, and 📝 links anything. Following a link takes you to the item and makes it pulse, so you can see it among the others.
- **The Day Planner looks like paper.** Pick notebook, dot journal, techie or glass. Drag tasks onto times, drag an item's bottom edge to set how long it takes, and see a marker at the time now. Copy the day as text, rich text or a WhatsApp message.
- **Brain dumps that become things without disappearing.** See above: that's the heart of it.
- **Your server, your keys.** Sync runs through a small server you run at home or on a cheap VPS. Everything is encrypted on your device first, so the server only ever holds scrambled copies. There's no account with anyone else, and it works offline.
- **Made for people who get overwhelmed.** It lets you let things go without guilt (the Day Planner has a "Let go" button), has down-day reminders, and never asks you to file anything before you've written it.
