# Sift

Sift isn't for sale. This page says what it's for, and what it does that the usual apps don't.

## In short

Sift is a personal organiser for people whose heads are busy: a place to empty your thoughts fast, then turn them into tasks, plans, contacts and things you can find again, without ever having to tidy up first. It works on a phone and a laptop, offline, installed from the browser like an app. It syncs between your devices through a small server you own, and everything is encrypted before it leaves your device.

It's built around one idea: **getting things out of your head should cost nothing, and sorting them out should never lose anything.**

## What it does

- **Brain Dump**: write anything, instantly. Notes you can turn into tasks, plans, contacts or stored things, and the note stays, linked to what it became.
- **Tasks**: lists for Now, Next and Later, sub-tasks, dates, time estimates, energy levels, projects, recurring tasks, and a running record of what actually happened on each one.
- **Day Planner**: your day on paper. Give tasks times and lengths, see your energy and focus for the day, carry unfinished things over, and let things go without guilt.
- **Lists**: shopping, packing, templates you can reuse.
- **Find Things**: where things are kept, down to the box and shelf, with photos and search.
- **Contacts and Cases**: every call, letter and task about one saga (a complaint, a claim, a repair) in a single timeline.
- **Search everything**: one box searches every note, task, comment, contact and list.

## The standout features

### Undo that remembers yesterday

In most apps, undo forgets everything the moment you close the note. In Sift, keep pressing **Ctrl+Z** in a note and it steps back through what you just typed, then carries on back through the note's earlier versions: the change you made yesterday, or last week, **even if you made it on a different device**. Ctrl+Y takes you forward again. On an iPhone, shake to undo does the same. There's also a list of a note's earlier versions with dates, to jump straight back to one. You choose how long history is kept.

### Brain dumps that become things without disappearing

When a line in a note becomes a task, a contact or a plan for the day, the note isn't emptied or thrown away. The words stay where you wrote them and turn into a link to what they became, and that task or contact links back. You never have to decide whether it's safe to delete the note.

### Nothing is lost by tidying up

Every change can be undone, in any order, from History. Archived things stay searchable. The Bin keeps deleted things for 30 days. Cut all the text out of a note and click away, and Sift asks whether you meant to empty it, rather than quietly doing either.

### A record of what actually happened

Any task can collect dated comments: "rang them, need their reference number", "done, cost £40". Tick a task off and you can add a closing comment in one tap. A comment can become a sub-task or a plan for today, set a day to check back, hold photos and files, and turn up in search and in the case it belongs to. Calls logged against a contact can go on the task too.

### Plans that happen by themselves

Give a task a day and it's on that day's plan; change the day and it moves. Make a task recurring ("put the bins out, every Tuesday") and ticking it makes the next one, on the right day, with its checklist ready again. Missed ones never pile up.

### Planning around your energy

Tell the Day Planner how you feel today and it suggests tasks that fit. Tasks carry an energy level and a time estimate, so a low day gets low-effort things, and down-day reminders help you do less without guilt.

### It looks like paper, and it's yours

The Day Planner comes as glass, notebook, dot journal, techie or minimal paper. Themes change the whole app's colours and fonts. Every name and heading in the app can be changed to your own words. Notes keep their formatting everywhere they appear: bold, crossed out, five text sizes, bullet lists, links to anything.

### Your server, your keys

Sync runs through a small server you run at home or on a cheap VPS. Everything is encrypted on your device first, so the server only ever holds scrambled copies. There's no account with anyone else, and it works fully offline. Edits from two devices merge field by field, so neither overwrites the other; text syncs before big files, so a slow photo never holds up your plan.

### Made for people who get overwhelmed

It never asks you to file anything before you've written it. Menus stay out of the way until they're useful. Pasting a screenshot into a note attaches it. Dropping a file anywhere tells you where it can go. Everything is one tap from being archived.

## Compared with Apple Notes and Google Keep

Apple Notes and Google Keep are good at keeping notes. Sift is for what happens to a note after you've written it.

| | Apple Notes | Google Keep | Sift |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quick capture, checklists, photos | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Undo past this visit, even edits made yesterday on another device | No | No | Yes |
| Turn a line into a task or a contact, and keep the line | No (copy it by hand) | No (a reminder at most) | Yes, and the line becomes a link |
| A phone number in a note is a real contact, with a call log | Tap to call only | Tap to call only | Yes |
| Your day on paper: tasks given times and lengths, unfinished things carried over | No | No | Day Planner |
| Recurring tasks that land on the right day | No | Reminders only | Yes |
| A running record of what happened on each task | No | No | Comments |
| Energy levels: choose today's tasks by how you feel | No | No | Yes |
| Where things are kept (boxes, shelves, search inside a box) | No | No | Find Things |
| Ongoing sagas: every call, letter and task about one complaint or claim in one timeline | No | No | Cases |
| Where your data lives | Apple's iCloud (end-to-end encryption if you turn it on) | Google's servers | Your devices, and optionally your own server, always end-to-end encrypted |
| Your own words for the app's names and headings | No | No | Yes |
